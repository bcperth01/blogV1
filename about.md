"### About the site owner
I've been associated with computers and communications technology since the mid 1970s.

In the last 7 years I have doing a fair bit of web development, both hobby and paid work.

### Web Development Landscape

Browsers can only interpret JavaScript, HTML and CSS. Nevertheless, web development encompasses myriads of languages, frameworks, libraries, tech stacks, on-line services and AI tools. Development and deployment is a rapidly moving feast

This means we are constantly navigating learning curves and problem solving. It is increasingly difficult for us - not to mention for AIs - to pick the optimum set of tools for any given job.

### Motivation

I have two reasons for building the site:

1. As a searchable ""knowledge base"" of solutions and interesting stuff that arises and accumulates during development, and so help stop re-inventing the wheel.
2. To write and collect bigger picture articles that are relevant to changing development/deployment landscape. I want these articles to be based on practical experience rather than guesswork or marketing ""puffery"".

In the first category, I will include small articles based on notes and scraps that I have accumulated.

In the second category I will describe the building of this web site, where I have gone out of may way to use the simplest and most battled hardened tools. Part of this will be assessing where more advanced tools would have helped, or the opposite!

Later I will include sites that progressively adopt more complex tools, including AI, and assess the learning curves and the effectiveness of the tools.

In the second category I will also include articles on broader topics that affect or underpin web development and deployment. Here I would seek contributions from others.

# TLDR

### Historical

Back in 1980, because of the exponential growth in hardware power, many people were convinced that all computer programs would be AI generated by 2000, and so herald the death of programming.

Just specify what you wanted your system to do and the AI would program it up for you.

What actually happened was an exponential growth in the number of programmers and the number of languages, frameworks, libraries and tech stacks ... all of them solving the same problems and all producing JavaScript, HTML and CSS as output.

### Today (2025)

In the last 10 years the big advance has been cloud computing. This started as virtual machines and networks and rapidly expanded to include services such as authorisation, databases, serverless functions, messaging systems, deployment tools, containerisation and so on.

More recently there has been an explosion of on-line services (SaaS) that leverage the cloud based infrastructure provided by companies like Amazon, Google and Microsoft. These can be consumed as API's by the programmer.

SaaS also be consumed directly by the end-user in the form of site-builders like Wordpress and many similar, where you can build brochure sites, blog sites and even include other services like Stripe, all without programming. You can ever get a leg up with some AI generated content.

### Large Language Models

Most recently, large language model ""AIs"" have come on the scene. These are still ""mostly harmless"" as software engineers, but will rapidly head in the direction of being able to accept a set of specifications and produce credible code in any desired language - the dream from 1980.

Its critical to stay alert, and not be fooled that any actual intelligence is involved, other than that of the engineers who conceived the LLM's and figured how to train them. AI's are still brute 100% force processing. Still NO SIGN WHATSOEVER of anything that could be remotely described as general intelligence!

### Impact on Software Engineers

We could imagine that the demand for junior web programmers will wane, as the ability of LLH's to produce correct code snippets improves. It's way too early to go down that path for many reasons that will be discussed in a later article.

The landscape continues to change - as it has for the past 50 years - so the daily activities of a software professional, and the tool set he uses, will continue to change. The system programmer/administrator of 2015, is the dev-ops engineer of 2025. The applications programmer of 1990 is the full stack developer of 2025.

People have invested huge sums in existing systems - there are still armies of Cobal programmers out there - and these systems will need to be maintained for years to come. Commercial interests can add inertia, slowing down the pace of change.

As the ""bricklaying"" is progressively automated the barriers to entry for computing professionals will increase. So teachers and engineers alike must adapt quickly to stay abreast - or risk becoming irrelevant. The emphasis will be on higher skills such as training and deploying AIs, design, testing, security, scaling, deployment, estimation, project management, cost optimisation and so on.

I suspect that to sustain the industry in the long term and in the face of progressive automation, that conceptual understanding - divorced from implementation - will become ever more relevant. Remember, the AI ""understands nothing"".

### Motivation

The aim of this site is to track the progress of system development tools, especially frameworks and AI related tools. I will do so by building small sites, progressively using more modern and complex tools and services, and assessing their effectiveness and learning effort and any other factors. The intention is to write articles that describe the experience."
